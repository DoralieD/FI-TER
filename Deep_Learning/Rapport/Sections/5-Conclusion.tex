\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

This work addressed the limited availability of accessible medical datasets for Deep Learning in healthcare by proposing a rule-based synthetic data generation framework for multimodal IoMT-inspired triage, combining 500,000 synthetic patient records with an external empirical validation step and a three-model benchmark spanning both deep and tree-based tabular paradigms.

Two methodological risks identified during development were directly addressed rather than left unexamined. First, generating data from rules alone offers no guarantee of clinical realism; comparing our synthetic vitals against 143,582 real Emergency Department admissions showed that central tendencies (SpO2, Heart Rate, Respiratory Rate) are well reproduced, while blood pressure variance is underestimated because real triage measurements exhibit a digit-preference rounding pattern that a continuous generator does not reproduce -- a concrete, quantified, and now explicit boundary of the framework rather than an untested assumption. Second, imputing missing laboratory tests with zero risked teaching the models to recognize test-ordering patterns instead of physiology; switching to median imputation removed this shortcut, and the resulting agreement between two structurally unrelated explainability methods (gradient-based SHAP on the neural network and tree-based SHAP on XGBoost) on the same top clinical drivers indicates that the classifiers rely on genuine physiological signal under the corrected preprocessing.

The benchmark itself confirms that the dataset is neither trivial nor degenerate: high macro ROC-AUC and Average Precision coexist with more moderate Accuracy and Macro F1 because residual errors concentrate on diagnosis pairs that are genuinely ambiguous at the point of triage, mirroring the kind of uncertainty a clinician would also face before further examination. Among the three models, no single architecture dominates every metric: the Transformer attains the highest Accuracy and ROC-AUC, while XGBoost achieves the highest Precision and Macro F1 at a training cost roughly an order of magnitude lower, confirming that a well-tuned tree-based baseline remains highly competitive with deep architectures on this structured tabular task; the MLP follows closely behind XGBoost on both accuracy and efficiency, whereas the Transformer's quadratic attention cost makes it the least suitable of the three for resource-constrained, edge-oriented IoMT deployment despite its raw accuracy edge.

The framework nonetheless has clear limitations. The generated records remain structured tabular representations rather than including unstructured modalities such as radiological images or free-text notes; the Chest X-ray and Brain CT variables are encoded findings, not images. The dataset also represents a static triage snapshot rather than a longitudinal trajectory, and does not yet model the temporal evolution of vitals or laboratory values. Finally, external validation was limited to seven vital-sign variables shared with the reference dataset; laboratory biomarkers such as Troponin, BNP, and D-Dimers could not be cross-checked against a public source with matching fields, and the transferability of models trained purely on synthetic data to real clinical deployment remains to be established.

Future work will pursue three directions accordingly. First, validating laboratory biomarkers against a richer clinical resource, such as a credentialed extract of MIMIC-IV-ED, would close the remaining gap left by the vitals-only comparison performed here. Second, injecting measurement-realistic noise (e.g., digit-preference rounding) into the vital-sign generator could narrow the residual distributional gap observed on blood pressure. Third, extending the framework with longitudinal IoMT signals and unstructured modalities (synthetic imaging combined with tabular data through CNN or Vision Transformer fusion) would move it toward a more complete multimodal diagnostic benchmark for healthcare research.
